# Related work and positioning

This focused note uses primary papers and the official GEPA repository, checked on 2026-09-17. It establishes conceptual relationships; it is not an exhaustive priority search or a claim of empirical superiority. Implementation details below refer to the [recorded method](FULL_CORPUS_METHOD.md), not to results from the ongoing run.

**Reflective optimization.** Agrawal et al., *GEPA: Reflective Prompt Evolution Can Outperform Reinforcement Learning* (2025), already combine trace-based textual reflection, minibatch improvement screening, per-example Pareto selection, ancestry, and system-aware crossover. These mechanisms belong to GEPA. Its official repository explicitly supports custom evaluation and reflective-data adapters. [Paper §3, Algorithms 1–2 and Appendix D.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.19457); [official repository, adapter interface](https://github.com/gepa-ai/gepa#readme).

Here, the optimized object is a jointly editable history router, state codebook, and explicit operation graph. A recorded rollout supplies the evaluation unit. Graph-specific additions include atomic node/edge patches, identity-indexed non-null training-history retention, and semantic reconciliation of graph candidates. That reconciliation is an LLM rewrite of interdependent definitions and endpoints, whereas the paper's merge constructs candidates by choosing module versions using their ancestry. These are adaptations around GEPA's search machinery. A custom adapter or a new application alone does not establish a new general optimizer or priority over all graph-induction methods.

**What a state abstraction preserves.** Li, Walsh, and Littman, *Towards a Unified Theory of State Abstraction for MDPs* (2006), distinguish abstractions preserving the one-step reward/transition model, action values, and optimal actions, and analyze their different planning and learning guarantees. This makes the preserved property part of the abstraction's definition, rather than something implied by grouping similar states. [Paper §§3.3–3.5, Definition 3 and Theorems 2–6](https://ai.dmi.unibas.ch/research/reading_group/li-et-al-isaim2006.pdf).

Our intended invariant concerns local decision situations and operation applicability across full histories. The system neither fits an abstract reward/transition distribution nor enforces value or optimal-action equivalence. Consequently, semantic coherence and observed path coverage do not establish bisimulation, a Markov representation, or policy-preservation guarantees. Post hoc terminal-reward variance describes the collected trajectories; it does not supply those missing guarantees.

**Generalization by merging observed structure.** Stolcke and Omohundro, *Inducing Probabilistic Grammars by Bayesian Model Merging* (1994), first incorporate samples into a model and then merge substructures. Their posterior objective trades likelihood against model simplicity; the HMM example starts with a separate path for each sample. [Paper §§2–3.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/cmp-lg/9409010).

The relevant precedent is that trace fit alone permits memorization, while merging can create reusable structure and new accepted sequences. Our semantic membership/edge scores and text-length penalty serve a different, heuristic objective. We do not optimize the paper's posterior or implement its statistical induction procedure. Reusing node labels also does not by itself demonstrate meaningful compression or valid new compositions.

**Process discovery from event logs.** Van der Aalst, Weijters, and Maruster, *Workflow Mining: Discovering Process Models from Event Logs* (2004), infer workflow models represented as Petri nets. Their rediscovery results require specified structural conditions and a log complete with respect to relevant directly-following relations; the paper also exhibits processes that cannot be distinguished or recovered by those relations alone. [Author-hosted paper, Definition 3.5 and §4](https://www.vdaalst.com/publications/p245.pdf).

Our recorded-prefix transitions similarly provide evidence for graph structure, but the nodes are learned descriptions of agent knowledge and blockers, rather than supplied event labels or Petri-net places. We do not establish complete logs or workflow-net soundness. A witnessed edge is existential evidence. The intended every-source-history applicability contract remains a hypothesis evaluated through semantic checks and sampled audits, not a consequence of observing that edge.

**Reusable workflows from agent experience.** Wang, Mao, Fried, and Neubig, *Agent Workflow Memory* (2024), induce reusable subroutines from trajectories, replace instance-specific entities with variables, and place the resulting workflows in agent memory. Their workflow steps include natural-language state descriptions, reasoning, and executable actions; induction supports offline and online settings. [Paper §§2.2–2.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.07429v1).

This is a direct precedent for language-based abstraction of agent experience and entity substitution. Our representation instead assigns individual full history prefixes to a shared codebook and evaluates explicit edges between those states for subsequent task construction. The current study does not evaluate a workflow-memory-augmented learner, so AWM's task-solving improvements cannot be inferred from a graph score or a successfully executed synthetic example.

The defensible contribution framing is **GEPA-based induction and auditing of a reusable local-state graph from recorded agent histories**, with an explicit coverage-retention rule and graph-aware editing. Any stronger claim about universal composability, faithful long-term abstraction, a generally better optimizer, or novelty priority requires evidence beyond these conceptual distinctions.
