# Research claims and interpretation checks

This note reviews the frozen full-corpus method and implementation, not run outcomes. It was written during the initial Pareto evaluation without inspecting Pareto/test feedback, changing the optimizer, or supplying manual critiques to reflection. See [FULL_CORPUS_METHOD.md](FULL_CORPUS_METHOD.md) for the protocol.

## Defensible claims and their boundaries

**Full-prefix formation.** Every deployed history receives a separate routing call containing its complete recorded prefix. The system jointly optimizes explicit state definitions, routing instructions, and edge specifications from recorded trajectories. This establishes the input and procedure; it does not establish sufficient statistics, behavioral equivalence, Markov states, correct membership, or identical continuation difficulty. Initialization samples local transitions; exhaustive final routing does not mean every training rollout influenced optimization. The rollout judge sees the complete numbered trajectory and is instructed to respect prefix boundaries, so judge hindsight leakage is not structurally excluded as it is for routing.

**Frozen proxy optimization.** The scalar combines judge-supported membership and observed-transition fractions, coherence, outgoing applicability, and a character-length penalty. Minibatch averages aggregate the same per-rollout rule. A gain supports improvement on this fixed proxy under the measured split and models. It does not alone establish cross-task reuse, an optimal number of nodes/edges, human interpretability, or executable composition. The complexity term penalizes serialized length, not directly the number of states or a formal information-theoretic objective. Prompt-separated evaluation with the same teacher/model family can share systematic errors with formation.

**Training-memory retention.** Acceptance preserves non-null assignments for the history identities previously classified on training rollouts encountered by the parent; crossover preserves the union of both parents' classified sets. Labels may change. Prior membership and transition judgments are not hard constraints: semantic quality remains assessed by the fixed score. Coverage is not correctness, and an incorrect prior assignment must be corrected by reassignment rather than abstention. This is an evolving evidence ledger, not a corpus-wide invariant, monotonic semantic accuracy, or monotonic Pareto/test performance. Ordinary mutations require strict training-minibatch improvement; crossover uses a different acceptance screen—at least the better parent's sum on a small Pareto subset—before retention and full Pareto evaluation. Pareto is adaptive development data, including this crossover use. This gate was declared before the first production proposal to match the classification-coverage requirement; no empirical comparison against the earlier, unexercised positive-judgment-retention design is claimed.

**Edge applicability.** The intended contract is every source member admitting some coherent target realization through the same bounded operation, with allowed bindings. Observed transitions establish witnesses. A few source checks, even all positive, supply empirical support only; they do not certify every corpus member or unseen history. Unknowns and counterexamples must remain visible. Endpoint-compatible graph paths additionally require consistent carried resources, constraints, and bindings; a path in the graph alone does not certify a task. “Traversable” in saved final artifacts means sampled drafting eligibility.

**Two distinct graph results.** Frozen seed-versus-selected test results apply to the pre-completion specifications. The later graph uses all splits to fill null assignments and reconstruct contracts over observed endpoint pairs. Earlier non-null assignments are preserved rather than repaired. Its exhaustive coverage is a transductive corpus description, not heldout generalization. The optimized edge specification is retained separately from reconstructed final contracts: final graph quality cannot be attributed solely to GEPA's edge edits. Test evaluation must never trigger another selection or prompt update in the same claimed test run.

**Outcome variance.** Repeating one terminal reward at many visited prefixes does not create independent outcomes. Report history-weighted, trajectory-deduplicated, and task-balanced summaries with their denominators. These describe outcomes among trajectories that visited the state under the source policy and task mixture; they do not identify per-history stochastic value variance, causal difficulty, or expected training benefit. Mixed tasks, different stages, and visit selection can produce high pooled variance. Bootstrap intervals condition on the learned graph and recorded outcomes; they omit graph-learning and policy-generation uncertainty. Keep the manually assigned reward sensitivity separate.

**Method contribution.** A defensible description is “GEPA-based joint graph induction with historical coverage retention and semantic crossover.” Explicit graph mutation, evidence bookkeeping, and retention are implementation/method contributions. Calling this a fundamentally new GEPA algorithm or claiming priority requires a literature comparison and evidence that those changes matter. Useful future comparisons include the same-budget seed, reflection without retention, no crossover, and simpler workflow clustering; this run alone does not establish their relative value.

## Final-report checklist

- [ ] Report actual proposals, accepted revisions, crossover attempts, stopping reason, and inference cost; distinguish ceilings from completed work.
- [ ] Report the frozen seed and selected specification, all test score components, paired task differences, and task-bootstrap intervals. The 150 test rollouts contain 15 task clusters, not 150 independent tasks; semantic overlap between original tasks may remain.
- [ ] Identify the model revisions, decoding policy, fixed evaluator, and adaptive Pareto role. Single-run intervals do not quantify selection or model-response uncertainty.
- [ ] Separate the frozen optimized graph, staged assignment completion, observed quotient edges, reconstructed contracts, and sampled-supported edges.
- [ ] Report state/edge counts, description length, task diversity, null assignments, contradictions, unknowns, failed checks, and sampling coverage alongside positive examples.
- [ ] Keep descriptive reward variation separate from local-cluster validity and conditional difficulty. Include trajectory/task counts and manual-outcome sensitivity.
- [ ] Separate generated drafts, semantic feasibility judgments, locally executed synthetic fixtures, official benchmark execution, and learner evaluation. Agreement between two independently prompted SQL solutions is a consistency check, not a proof of task fidelity.
- [ ] Present the independent seed review as qualitative analysis, not gold labels or accuracy. Claim neither universal composability nor learner improvement without the corresponding evidence.
